# Osteoarthritis Management Guidelines

## Conservative Therapy Requirements

### Initial Management Protocol

Pre-authorization for surgical interventions requires documentation of ALL conservative treatments attempted for minimum timeframes:

1. **Non-Pharmacological Interventions (Minimum 6 months)**
   - Structured physical therapy program (≥ 12 sessions over 3 months)
   - Weight management counseling and documented weight loss attempt (BMI ≥ 25)
   - Patient education program including joint protection techniques
   - Use of assistive devices (canes, braces, orthotics) as appropriate [1]

2. **Pharmacological Management (Minimum 3 months)**
   - Adequate trial of oral NSAIDs (unless contraindicated)
   - Topical NSAIDs for knee and hand osteoarthritis
   - Paracetamol/acetaminophen as first-line analgesic
   - Tramadol for moderate to severe pain unresponsive to other treatments [2]

3. **Intra-articular Therapy (If Applicable)**
   - Corticosteroid injections (maximum 3-4 per year per joint)
   - Hyaluronic acid injections for knee osteoarthritis (series completion)
   - Documentation of temporary symptom improvement with injections
   - Clear rationale for discontinuation or failure of injectable treatments [3]

### Physical Therapy Requirements

#### Comprehensive Assessment
- Baseline functional assessment using validated outcome measures:
  - Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Arthritis Index (WOMAC)
  - Knee injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score (KOOS) for knee OA
  - Hip disability and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score (HOOS) for hip OA
  - Visual Analog Scale (VAS) for pain assessment [4]

#### Structured Exercise Program
- Supervised strengthening exercises focusing on affected joint muscle groups
- Range of motion exercises to maintain joint flexibility
- Aerobic conditioning appropriate for patient's functional level
- Balance and proprioception training for lower extremity osteoarthritis
- Home exercise program with demonstrated patient compliance [5]

#### Functional Training
- Activities of daily living training and adaptation techniques
- Occupational therapy assessment for workplace modifications
- Gait training and use of mobility aids as appropriate
- Joint protection education and energy conservation strategies [6]

## Imaging and Diagnostic Requirements

### Radiographic Criteria

#### Standard Imaging Protocol
- Weight-bearing anteroposterior and lateral radiographs of affected joint
- Kellgren-Lawrence grading scale documentation (Grade 0-4)
- Joint space narrowing measurement and progression documentation
- Presence of osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis, and cysts
- Comparison with previous imaging to demonstrate progression [7]

#### Advanced Imaging Indications
- MRI indicated for:
  - Suspected meniscal tears in knee osteoarthritis
  - Evaluation of cartilage damage severity
  - Assessment of bone marrow edema
  - Pre-surgical planning for joint replacement [8]

### Functional Assessment Requirements

#### Objective Functional Measures
- 6-minute walk test for lower extremity osteoarthritis
- Timed up-and-go test for mobility assessment
- Stair climbing test (if applicable to patient's daily activities)
- Grip strength measurement for hand osteoarthritis
- Joint-specific range of motion measurements [9]

#### Quality of Life Assessment
- EuroQol-5D (EQ-5D) health status questionnaire
- Short Form-36 (SF-36) or SF-12 health survey
- Arthritis Impact Measurement Scale (AIMS2)
- Patient Global Assessment of disease severity
- Sleep quality assessment using Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index [10]

## Surgical Intervention Criteria

### Total Joint Replacement Indications

#### Primary Indications for Total Hip Replacement
- Radiographic evidence of severe osteoarthritis (Kellgren-Lawrence Grade 3-4)
- Persistent pain significantly impacting daily activities despite conservative management
- Functional limitation affecting work, recreation, or activities of daily living
- Failed conservative treatment for minimum 6 months (12 months if age < 65 years)
- Absence of active infection or medical contraindications to surgery [11]

#### Primary Indications for Total Knee Replacement  
- Kellgren-Lawrence Grade 3-4 osteoarthritis with joint space narrowing
- Pain severity ≥ 7/10 on VAS despite optimal medical management
- WOMAC pain subscale score ≥ 40/100 or function subscale score ≥ 35/100
- Significant functional impairment with validated outcome measures
- Realistic expectations for surgical outcomes and rehabilitation commitment [12]

### Partial Joint Replacement Criteria

#### Unicompartmental Knee Replacement Indications
- Isolated medial or lateral compartment osteoarthritis
- Intact anterior cruciate ligament with good stability
- Minimal deformity (valgus < 10°, varus < 15°, flexion contracture < 15°)
- Good range of motion (≥ 90° flexion, < 10° fixed flexion deformity)
- Age < 75 years with high activity level and good bone quality [13]

#### Hip Resurfacing Arthroplasty Criteria
- Age < 65 years with high functional demands
- Good bone quality without cysts or significant osteoporosis
- Preserved femoral head geometry with minimal deformity
- No history of femoral neck fracture or avascular necrosis
- Male gender preferred due to higher success rates [14]

### Arthroscopic Intervention Guidelines

#### Knee Arthroscopy Indications
- Mechanical symptoms with confirmed meniscal tear on MRI
- Loose body removal causing locking or catching symptoms
- Limited to patients < 50 years with minimal osteoarthritic changes
- NOT indicated for osteoarthritis without mechanical symptoms
- Clear documentation of mechanical component to patient's symptoms [15]

#### Hip Arthroscopy Criteria
- Femoroacetabular impingement with appropriate morphology
- Labral tears in patients < 40 years with minimal osteoarthritic changes
- Failed conservative management for minimum 3 months
- Preserved joint space and acetabular cartilage on imaging
- Realistic expectations for symptom improvement [16]

## Age-Specific Considerations

### Young Adult Osteoarthritis (Age < 50 years)
- Enhanced emphasis on joint preservation procedures
- Biological treatments consideration (PRP, stem cell therapy under research protocols)
- Occupational assessment and workplace modification
- Long-term joint replacement planning and staged procedures
- Genetic counseling for familial osteoarthritis patterns [17]

### Elderly Osteoarthritis (Age ≥ 75 years)
- Comprehensive geriatric assessment including cognitive function
- Falls risk assessment and prevention strategies
- Medication interaction review and renal function monitoring
- Multidisciplinary team approach including geriatrician
- Modified surgical criteria based on life expectancy and functional goals [18]

## Comorbidity Management

### Obesity and Osteoarthritis
- BMI ≥ 30 requires structured weight management program
- Bariatric surgery consideration for BMI ≥ 40 with failed weight loss attempts
- Nutritionist consultation and dietary modification program
- Exercise program modification for weight-bearing limitations
- Documentation of weight loss attempts prior to surgical consideration [19]

### Diabetes and Osteoarthritis Management
- HbA1c optimization (< 7.5%) prior to surgical procedures
- Enhanced infection prevention protocols
- Wound healing assessment and diabetic foot evaluation
- Coordination with endocrinology for perioperative glucose management
- Modified rehabilitation protocols for diabetic complications [20]

## Economic Evaluation and Cost-Effectiveness

### Treatment Cost Analysis
- Conservative treatment costs vs. surgical intervention over 5-year period
- Quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) improvement analysis
- Return to work and productivity assessment
- Healthcare utilization reduction following effective treatment
- Long-term cost savings from improved functional outcomes [21]

### Resource Utilization Guidelines
- Appropriate use of imaging studies to prevent unnecessary testing
- Physical therapy session optimization based on response patterns
- Medication cost-effectiveness analysis including generic alternatives
- Surgical volume and outcome correlation for provider selection
- Post-operative rehabilitation resource allocation [22]

## Monitoring and Outcome Assessment

### Short-term Follow-up (3-6 months)
- Pain reduction assessment using VAS or numeric rating scale
- Functional improvement using joint-specific outcome measures
- Patient satisfaction with treatment interventions
- Adverse effects monitoring for pharmacological treatments
- Adherence assessment for physical therapy and exercise programs [23]

### Long-term Follow-up (Annual)
- Radiographic progression assessment
- Functional status maintenance or improvement
- Quality of life outcome measures
- Need for additional interventions or treatment modifications
- Cost-effectiveness analysis of treatment approach [24]

### Surgical Outcome Monitoring
- Hospital length of stay and early complication rates
- 30-day and 90-day readmission rates
- Patient-reported outcome measures at 6 months and annually
- Implant survivorship and revision rates
- Return to work and activity levels post-surgery [25]
